# Customer letter

> **Working-backwards document.** This is the letter we would want a customer to write **after** using the Receiving Manager for a quarter. No customer has been interviewed. The person and warehouse are invented, and so are any events described. It states the outcome we're building towards, so we can check the build against it.

---

To the Receiving Manager team,

I run the inbound dock at a 3PL. We take supplier deliveries for about forty sellers, most of it sea freight from overseas manufacturers. My receivers used to open a carton or two per pallet, eyeball it, and sign the delivery note. If something was wrong we usually found out weeks later, when prep flagged a missing part or a customer sent a return. By then the supplier's answer was always the same: "prove it arrived like that." We couldn't.

What changed is that every delivery now leaves a record while the goods are still on the dock. My receivers take three or four photos per PO line and type the carton count. The screen shows each check with a reason: right product, right count, no damage, right variant, parts present. The part I didn't expect to like is that it says **"can't tell"** when it can't tell. A dark photo or a carton photographed from the wrong side doesn't get waved through. It goes to the review queue and someone looks. My team trusts it more because it doesn't pretend.

When a supplier short-shipped a line, we had the record: the PO line, the count, the photo of the open carton, the time, who counted it, and the reason the agent flagged it. The seller sent that to the supplier the same week. That conversation used to be unwinnable.

Two things I still want. It is slow when the internet is down, although it never blocks us: the photos are saved and the check runs later. And the colour matching sometimes gets fussy with shades.

Receiving lead, a 3PL
